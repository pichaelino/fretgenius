Circle of fifths

The twelve keys arranged so each neighbour is a fifth apart. Tap a key to see its signature, its chords and its closest relatives.

How the circle works

Reading it

Go clockwise and every step goes up a perfect fifth (seven frets): C, G, D, A and on around. Go counter-clockwise and each step goes up a fourth instead. After twelve steps you are back at C, which is why it closes into a circle.

Sharps and flats

Each clockwise step adds one sharp to the key signature, and each counter-clockwise step adds one flat. G has one sharp, D has two, F has one flat, B♭ has two. At the bottom, F♯ and G♭ are the same notes with different names.

Relative minors

The inner ring shows the minor key that shares the same notes as the major key beside it. A minor and C major use exactly the same seven notes; the difference is which note feels like home. The relative minor always sits three frets below the major.

Building progressions

The selected key and its two neighbours give you the six most useful chords: I, IV and V on the outside, and ii, iii and vi on the inside. Almost every pop, rock and folk song in that key lives in this little wedge, plus the diminished vii°.

Changing key

Keys next to each other share six of their seven notes, so moving one step around the circle is the smoothest key change. Jumping across the circle (C to F♯) sounds dramatic because the keys share almost nothing.

Hearing the pull

Chord movement counter-clockwise (G to C, D to G) is the strongest resolution in music: the V–I cadence. Chains like E7–A7–D7–G7–C walk counter-clockwise around the circle and are the backbone of jazz and ragtime.

On the guitar

Guitar-friendly keys cluster at the top right: G, D, A and E, with their open strings. A capo shifts everything up the circle's chromatic order, so you can play G shapes and sound in A with the capo at fret 2.

Spotting borrowed chords

Chords from one step counter-clockwise plus the parallel minor (♭VII, ♭VI, iv) are the classic "borrowed" colours in rock. The Song Builder flags them for you.
